Five days from Lone Pine to the summit. Halal, boil-water-only, built
around the one thing that actually goes wrong up high: you stop wanting
to eat.

| day | | elevation | eat | drink |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sep 13 | drive to Lone Pine | 3,700 ft | ~2,900 kcal | 3.5 L |
| sep 14 | Lone Pine Lake, Portal overnight | 8,360 ft | ~3,200 kcal | 4 L |
| sep 15 | carry to Trail Camp | 12,000 ft | ~3,000 kcal | 4.5 L |
| sep 16 | acclimatize | 12,000 ft | ~2,800 kcal | 4–5 L |
| sep 17 | summit and out | 14,505 ft | 250–300 g carbs | 5 L+ |

## the six rules that don't change

1. **Eat by the clock, not by hunger.** Above 10,000 ft appetite drops
   before energy demand does. Something every 45 minutes moving, every
   2 hours in camp.
2. **Carbs win at altitude.** Carbohydrate yields more energy per liter
   of oxygen than fat does. From Sept 15 on, 60–65% of calories from
   carbs; fat rides along for density, not as the main event.
3. **Fluid is a target, not a response.** 4–5 L/day above 10,000 ft —
   dry air steals over a liter a day through breathing alone. Pale
   straw urine every 2–3 hours is the check.
4. **Salt what you drink.** 500–800 mg sodium per liter on moving days.
   Water alone plus heavy sweat is how people get into trouble up there.
5. **The 30-minute window after you stop.** Appetite falls off a cliff
   once you sit down at camp. 60–80 g carbs plus 25 g protein *before*
   the boots come off.
6. **Nothing new above the Portal.** Every food here gets tested on a
   training hike first. Sept 17 is not the day to learn how your
   stomach feels about a new gel.

## day by day

### sept 13 — load the tank

| when | what | kcal |
|---|---|---|
| pre-drive | Oats, banana, two spoons of peanut butter, coffee | ~600 |
| lunch stop | Sandwich or burrito, salted. Sit-down beats gas-station grazing | ~600 |
| on the road | Something every two hours — dates, pretzels, a juice. Keep it carby | ~700 |
| Lone Pine | Rice or pasta dinner, moderate protein, low grease. Salt it heavily | ~1,000 |
| bedtime | 500 ml water with an electrolyte tab | — |

Alcohol is off from tonight through the summit — it blunts
acclimatization and wrecks altitude sleep.

### sept 14 — last easy meals

| when | what | kcal |
|---|---|---|
| breakfast | Eat big at the BnB — eggs, toast, fruit, coffee | ~800 |
| on trail | 40–60 g carbs/hr: one bar plus chews. Practice the timer habit here | ~500 |
| back at camp | Wrap and a drink. **Appetite test** — see below | ~600 |
| dinner | Big carb dinner at the Portal | ~1,100 |
| bedtime | Three dates and a hot drink. Sleeping cold burns more than you think | ~200 |

**The appetite test.** If food already sounds unappealing at 8,360 ft,
plan to go liquid-heavy at Trail Camp — drink mixes and soup instead of
solid dinners.

If halal meat isn't confirmed at the Portal Store, the safe defaults
are pancakes, grilled cheese, and fries.

### sept 15 — the carry

6 mi, 3,700 ft of gain, full pack. Burns ~4,000; realistically ~3,000 goes in.

| when | what | kcal |
|---|---|---|
| 90 min out | Two oat packets, a scoop of protein, honey, coffee | ~600 |
| moving | 50–70 g carbs/hr, 500–700 ml/hr, 500–700 mg sodium/hr | ~900 |
| arrival | Within 30 min of dropping the pack: recovery drink or ramen plus protein | ~400 |
| dinner | Freeze-dried vegetarian entrée, olive oil packet stirred in, tortilla | ~950 |
| bedtime | Hot cocoa with 30 g carbs. Treat tomorrow's water now, not at 2 am | ~200 |

Loaded pack on a scap that's been cranky — keep the hip belt carrying
the weight, and take the eating stops as scap resets too.

### sept 16 — the hard eating day

Low output, lowest appetite of the trip, still needs near 3,000. This
is the real fueling risk, not summit day.

| when | what | kcal |
|---|---|---|
| all day | Small amounts every two hours, by watch. Nothing greasy | ~550 |
| breakfast | Oats with protein and nut butter | ~650 |
| midday | Short walk toward Trail Crest — carry 200 kcal and actually eat it. Tortilla and nut butter back at camp | ~700 |
| dinner 17:30 | Early and bland — mac and cheese or couscous. Digestion done before a 02:00 wake-up | ~900 |

**If food fails, go liquid.** Drink mix, instant soup, cocoa with a
protein scoop. 600 drinkable calories beats 300 pushed around the bag.

Before bed, sort tomorrow's summit food into one jacket pocket. If you
have to open the pack in the dark and cold, you won't eat it.

### sept 17 — summit day

| time | what |
|---|---|
| 02:00 | Instant oats or cream of wheat, honey, half a protein scoop. Low fiber, low fat, 250–350 kcal. Coffee only if it's your normal habit. 500 ml with electrolyte |
| 02:45 | Depart. Pocket check: four gels or chew packs, two bars, one salty item, electrolyte tabs |
| 03:30 | First feed — before you feel like you need it. Then every 45 min: 25–30 g carbs. Target 60–75 g/hr |
| 05:00 | Trail Crest, 13,600 ft. Sit five minutes, eat a real 200 kcal item, drink properly. Past here eating gets harder and stopping gets colder |
| 07:30 | Summit. 150–200 kcal even if nauseous. Make it something you like — this is why the candy is in the pack |
| 08:00 | Descent. Keep 30 g/hr going. Most bonks on this mountain happen going down |
| 10:30 | Trail Camp. 400–500 kcal plus 25 g protein before breaking camp. Eat first, pack second |
| 14:00 | Portal. Finish what's left on the walk out, then a real meal in Lone Pine. Salt and carbs first, protein second |

## halal notes

Most backcountry food ignores it, so:

- **Freeze-dried entrées** — almost no meat versions are certified.
  Build every dinner on a vegetarian entrée and add protein with a
  powder scoop or a fish pouch. Simpler than hunting certification.
- **Jerky** — skip the standard stuff. Halal jerky exists but ships
  slowly; order by Sept 5 or leave it out and cover protein with
  cheese, nuts, and fish.
- **Chews and gummies** — gelatin is the trap. Pectin-based chews and
  nearly all gels are fine. Read the panel once, buy in bulk.
- **Cocoa mixes** — the ones with mini marshmallows contain gelatin.
  Buy plain.

## what to leave out

- Any food not tested on a training hike.
- Alcohol, Sept 12 through the summit.
- High-fat, high-fiber, or spicy meals from Sept 15 on — altitude
  already slows gastric emptying.
- Heavy protein loads at dinner above 10,000 ft. Protein is the hardest
  macro to digest up high.
- The 02:00 breakfast, on the grounds of not being hungry. You won't be.
  Eat it anyway.
